How it feels

Four moods.
One little notch.

01 — IDLE

Quiet. Just the time.

When nothing's playing, Crest shrinks to a tiny pill with the clock. No dancing bars, no distraction.

02 — PLAYING

Music starts. It wakes up.

The notch widens smoothly and the spectrum slides in next to the time. Pause, and after a moment it settles back down.

03 — PEEK

A new song says hello.

The notch pops open for a few seconds with the cover art and a scrolling title, then tucks itself away.

04 — LIVE ACTIVITY

It tells you what's up.

Plug in the charger, connect your headphones, a meeting is about to start, a timer ends — the notch pops open with just what you need to know.

05 — EXPANDED

Hover for everything.

The full player, plus pages for lyrics, calendar, weather, system stats, a timer, a file shelf and notes — swipe sideways to flip through.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Which music apps does it work with?

Anything that shows up in the Windows media flyout (the box that appears when you press a volume key): Spotify, YouTube and other sites in Edge, Chrome or Firefox, Windows Media Player, and most modern players.

Where do the lyrics and weather come from?

Lyrics come from LRCLIB and weather from Open-Meteo — both free and open, no account or API key. Crest only sends the song's title and artist, or the city you typed.

How does the calendar work? Is my data safe?

Paste your calendar's private iCal link (Google Calendar, Outlook and iCloud all have one) in Settings → Activities. Crest downloads it straight from your calendar provider and keeps it on your PC — nothing goes anywhere else. Clipboard history is off by default and never saved to disk.

Will it slow down my PC or my games?

No. When nothing is happening, every timer and animation stops, so it uses practically no CPU. The optional "real spectrum" uses a little more while music plays — it's off by default.

Does it stay visible in fullscreen?

Yes, above fullscreen videos, movies and borderless/windowed-fullscreen games. Games in true exclusive fullscreen own the whole display, so no app can draw over them — switch those to borderless.

How do I open settings or quit?

Right-click the notch, click the gear in the expanded player, or use the Crest icon in the system tray. The tray menu also has "Start with Windows" and "Exit".

Is it safe? Why the SmartScreen warning?

Crest is open source — you can read every line or build it yourself with one command. The warning appears for apps that aren't code-signed yet; it doesn't mean anything is wrong.
